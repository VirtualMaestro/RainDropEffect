# Performance

Measured and budgeted costs for Rain Drop Effect URP 2.0. Rows are appended as tasks measure them;
verified numbers are never rewritten.

## Render target budget

The pass allocates at most two transient textures per camera per frame:

- **the copy** — one full-resolution copy of the camera colour, taken before anything is drawn, so
  the lens can refract the scene without sampling its own attachment. Allocated only when a visible
  lens layer exists.
- **the blur** — a half-resolution horizontal blur of the same source. Allocated only when a visible
  lens layer has `Blur > 0` *and* the quality tier is not Low.

Both are Render Graph textures, so they are pooled and released with the frame; nothing is held
between frames. Layer count does not change these figures — one blur serves every layer on the
camera.

| Resolution | Colour format | Copy | Blur (half res) | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1280 × 720 | LDR (R8G8B8A8, 4 B/px) | 3.52 MiB | 0.88 MiB | **4.40 MiB** |
| 1920 × 1080 | LDR (R8G8B8A8, 4 B/px) | 7.91 MiB | 1.98 MiB | **9.89 MiB** |
| 1280 × 720 | HDR (R16G16B16A16, 8 B/px) | 7.03 MiB | 1.76 MiB | **8.79 MiB** |
| 1920 × 1080 | HDR (R16G16B16A16, 8 B/px) | 15.82 MiB | 3.96 MiB | **19.78 MiB** |

Against the research ceilings (16 MiB at 720p LDR, 32 MiB at 1080p LDR) the LDR configurations use
28 % and 31 % of the allowance. HDR doubles the per-pixel cost of both textures; a 1080p HDR camera
still fits inside the 1080p LDR ceiling with room to spare.

Overlay-only frames allocate neither texture: they draw straight into the camera attachment.
Frames with nothing visible enqueue no pass at all.

## Blur kernel

Nine horizontal taps with the legacy weights `{0.05, 0.09, 0.12, 0.15, 0.18, 0.15, 0.12, 0.09, 0.05}`,
sampled from the full-resolution copy while writing half resolution. The tap offset is
`texel.x × k × radius`, with the radius set by the quality tier:

| Quality | Blur | Radius (source pixels) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | disabled | — |
| Balanced | enabled | 4 |
| High | enabled | 6 |

Widened from 2/3 on 2026-09-10 (finding F1): the original kernel reached about a tenth as far as the
1.x blur it replaces. The tap count is fixed at nine, so a wider radius costs no extra samples — only
reach. The render-target budget above is unaffected.

Per-drop blur strength is `saturate(Blur × |n.x| × 16)`, so a drop with a flat normal is never
blurred no matter what the layer setting says.

### Blur tuning

**Retuned to `16` on 2026-09-10.** The first implementation barely blurred at all. `Frozen` was the
clearest case: its `frozenfill` layer is meant to smear the whole view, and the frame came back
sharp.

The retune alone was not enough. The larger cause was `Blur` being treated as a mix weight when it
has to be a kernel *width* with full replacement; that was corrected in the same iteration.

Per-drop blur strength is therefore now `saturate(Blur × |n.x| × 16)`.

## CPU cost

Measured by `DenseTrailBenchmarkTests` (PlayMode) over 300 consecutive tick + emit calls after a
120-frame warm-up, on the densest supported configuration: 256 simple drops, 32 flow trails and
32 friction trails, all at 96 trail points, quality High.

| Date | Machine | Configuration | Mean | p95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-09-10 | Intel Core i9-14900HX (editor) | dense: 256 drops + 32 flow + 32 friction @ 96 points | 0.452 ms | 0.616 ms |

This is a desktop editor figure and is not a device budget — it exists so Phase 6 optimization
decisions start from a number. Task 25 measures the real per-tier budgets on target hardware.

The simulation allocates no managed memory per frame after the first: `RainEffectAllocationTests`
and `TrailAllocationTests` assert that for the Basic profile, a 256-drop profile, and the dense trail
configuration above.

## Measured budgets (Task 25)

### Method

A Development player built from `Assets/Host/Benchmark.unity` by
`tools/run-benchmark.ps1`. Editor Play Mode is not a valid GPU measurement, so no number below comes
from one. The scene is generated by `BenchmarkSetup` so a re-run reproduces it exactly: 18 opaque
primitives and a floor, 20 transparent URP/Lit quads, one rotating cube, one camera with a
`RainEffect`.

Each scenario runs 60 settling frames, then 600 sampled frames. Per frame the driver records
`Time.unscaledDeltaTime` and, where the platform supports it,
`FrameTimingManager.GetLatestTimings` (`cpuFrameTime`, `gpuFrameTime`). It reports the p50 and p95
of each, plus `GC.CollectionCount(0)` and `Profiler.GetTotalAllocatedMemoryLong()` deltas across the
600 frames. VSync is off and the target frame rate is uncapped, so the frame time is the work, not
the refresh interval.

The three benchmark profiles ship in `Runtime/Profiles/` and are excluded from the gallery sample:

| Profile | Content |
| --- | --- |
| `Bench_Sparse` | one static frame layer + 32 simple drops |
| `Bench_Trails` | 16 flow trails (blur 0.5) + 16 friction trails, 64 points each |
| `Bench_Combined` | static frame + 256 simple drops + 32 flow + 32 friction, both trail families blurred |

Quality caps apply on top of the authored counts (drops 48/128/256, trails 8/16/32,
points 32/64/96 for Low/Balanced/High), which is why the Low rows are not simply the High rows minus
the blur.

### Against the research budgets

Native budgets: Low ≤ 2 ms GPU / ≤ 0.5 ms CPU, Balanced ≤ 3 / ≤ 0.75, High ≤ 4 / ≤ 1.

| Tier | GPU cost (Δ, worst profile, p95) | GPU budget | Used | CPU p95 (worst profile) | CPU budget | Used |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low | 0.065 ms | 2 ms | 3 % | 0.557 ms | 0.5 ms | 111 % |
| Balanced | 0.067 ms | 3 ms | 2 % | 0.587 ms | 0.75 ms | 78 % |
| High | 0.070 ms | 4 ms | 2 % | 0.588 ms | 1 ms | 59 % |

The CPU column is the **whole frame**, not the effect: the no-rain frame already costs 0.444 ms p95
on this machine because the benchmark scene itself is rendered every frame. The effect's own share
is the delta against `none`, which is at most 0.144 ms p95 (`Bench_Combined` High) — 14 % of the
High budget and 29 % of the Low one. Read that way no tier is over budget; the table above is left
in because the absolute frame cost is what a shipping game actually has to fit.

GC delta is 0 for every scenario on Windows, which is NFR-01's requirement.
`Profiler.GetTotalAllocatedMemoryLong()` moves by a few kilobytes in both directions between
scenarios (largest: +164 kB in `Bench_Trails`/Balanced) — that is the profiler's own total, not a
managed allocation, and the managed collector never ran.

### WebGL2 (Chrome)

The same benchmark scene built for WebGL2 (`Builds/Benchmark/WebGL`, IL2CPP, uncompressed) and
served over `http://localhost`. Two limits shape what a browser can tell us:
`FrameTimingManager` reports no GPU time there, so the JSON carries `gpuFrameTime: null` with
`note: "GPU timing unavailable on this platform"`; and the frame loop is `requestAnimationFrame`,
capped at the display refresh. The useful question is therefore not "how many milliseconds" but
"does it hold the refresh rate".

`Logs/benchmark-WebGL-1280x720.json`, 2026-09-10. Chrome on Windows 11, WebGL 2.0 through ANGLE
(`ANGLE (NVIDIA, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4080 Laptop GPU, Direct3D11 vs_5_0 ps_5_0, D3D11)`), IL2CPP,
canvas 960 × 600, uncompressed build served from `http://localhost`.

| Scenario | Quality | Frame p50 | Frame p95 | GPU | GC |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| none | — | 17.0 ms | 17.0 ms | n/a | 0 |
| Bench_Sparse | Low / Balanced / High | 17.0 ms | 17.0 ms | n/a | 0 |
| Bench_Trails | Low / Balanced / High | 17.0 ms | 17.0 ms | n/a | 0 |
| Bench_Combined | Low / Balanced / High | 17.0 ms | 17.0 ms | n/a | 0 |

Every scenario sits exactly on the 60 Hz refresh interval, p50 and p95 alike. That is the result,
not a missing measurement: `requestAnimationFrame` never let a frame run long, so **no scenario —
including `Bench_Combined` at High — dropped a single frame in 600**. What the browser cannot say is
how much headroom is left; a machine that could not hold the refresh would show p95 above 17 ms, and
none did.

GC delta is 0 for all ten scenarios on IL2CPP as well, which is the allocation claim tested on a
second runtime.

Console noise worth knowing about: Chrome reports `Hidden/CoreSRP/CoreCopy`,
`Hidden/Universal Render Pipeline/StencilDitherMaskSeed` and `Hidden/Universal/HDRDebugView` as
unsupported on WebGL. Those are URP's own debug and utility shaders, not this package's; the rain
renders correctly regardless.

**GAP:** GPU milliseconds. `FrameTimingManager` reports no GPU time in a browser, and deriving it
from the frame rate would be a guess.

### Pass counts

Derived from the pass topology (decision D8) and asserted by `RainRenderPassTests` and
`RainBlurPassTests` rather than read out of a Frame Debugger capture:

| Case | Extra render passes |
| --- | --- |
| no visible layer | 0 — the feature enqueues nothing |
| overlay-only | 1 raster pass into the camera attachment, no copy |
| lens, no blur | 1 blit (attachment → `C0`) + 1 raster |
| lens with blur (Balanced/High) | 1 blit (attachment → `C0`) + 1 blit (half-res blur) + 1 raster |

`Bench_Combined` at Balanced or High is the last row: **3 native passes** on top of the no-rain
frame, independent of layer count — one scene copy and one blur serve every layer on the camera.

**GAP:** a Frame Debugger / Render Graph Viewer capture of the same scene needs an interactive
editor session and was not taken.

### Sustained run (Task 31)

`tools/run-benchmark.ps1 -Sustained` runs `Bench_Combined` at `Balanced` for twelve minutes,
bucketing frame time every ten seconds along with the battery level and any focus change. The point
is not the average — the sweep above already has that — but whether minute 12 looks like minute 1
once the machine has warmed up.

`Logs/sustained-WindowsPlayer.json`, 2026-09-10. Same machine and player as the sweep,
`Bench_Combined` at `Balanced`, 1280 × 720, 710 s in 71 ten-second buckets, ~16 800 frames each.

| Bucket | Elapsed | Frame p50 | Frame p95 | GPU p50 | GPU p95 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 10 s | 0.541 ms | 0.763 ms | 0.146 ms | 0.171 ms |
| 2 | 30 s | 0.604 ms | 0.846 ms | 0.170 ms | 0.182 ms |
| 35 | 360 s | 0.565 ms | 0.769 ms | 0.164 ms | 0.209 ms |
| 69 | 700 s | 0.559 ms | 0.716 ms | 0.163 ms | 0.177 ms |
| 70 | 710 s | 0.559 ms | 0.731 ms | 0.159 ms | 0.172 ms |

Across all 71 buckets frame p95 stayed between 0.662 ms and 1.201 ms and GPU p50 between 0.132 ms
and 0.174 ms. The thermal gate asks that p95 not degrade by more than 30 % between minute 1 and
minute 12: the worst p95 in minute 1 is **0.846 ms** and in minute 12 **0.836 ms** — a 1 % *improvement*,
so nothing to record as a limitation. Battery level never moved (0.77, on mains). The window lost and
regained focus five times during the run without a burst on resume, which is the delta-time clamp
doing its job.

Two limits of this measurement, stated rather than hidden: it is a desktop GPU with an enormous
thermal budget, so it is evidence that nothing leaks or drifts over twelve minutes, not evidence
about a phone; and no mobile device was available to run the same pass on.
